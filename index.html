Gaze-Adaptive VR Streaming for Remote Operation of Autonomous Ferries

Kainat Altaf, Olaf Landsiedel
Kiel University (CAU) · Hamburg University of Technology (TUHH)
Look where it matters: stream the ferry’s cameras in full quality only where the remote operator looks, so immersive 360° VR supervision fits through narrow, fluctuating ship-to-shore links.

Motivation

  • Autonomous ferries on the Kiel Fjord are supervised from land.
  • Remote operators need live situational awareness: traffic, buoys, kayakers, swimmers, docking.
  • VR headsets give a 360° view around the ferry, but need high bitrates.
  • Mobile links at sea have no guaranteed bandwidth or latency.

Idea: Look Where It Matters

Problem: How do we stream immersive, low-latency 360° ship video over a fluctuating link without degrading what the operator looks at?

Solution: The eye sees sharply only in a tiny area (≈ 2°). Send full quality only where the operator looks and save bandwidth everywhere else.

Gaze-Adaptive Streaming over WebRTC

  • The VR headset’s eye tracker sends the live gaze point to the ship over a WebRTC data channel.
  • The edge encoder keeps full quality at the gaze point and lowers it in the periphery.
  • The foveated video returns over WebRTC to the headset.

Real Link Conditions

  • Coverage datasets: Kiel Fjord & Kiel Canal (Vodafone, Telekom 4G/5G; Starlink).
  • Cellular video delay spikes to 685 ms (p95); bridges briefly block Starlink (CanalLink).
  • The stream must adapt in real time.
